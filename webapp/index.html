How it works

Satellites have counted fire hotspots over this region since 2000, but the record is split. MODIS (on Terra and Aqua) sees 1 km pixels and has flown since 2000. VIIRS (on S-NPP since 2012, NOAA-20 since 2018, NOAA-21 since 2023) sees 375 m pixels and catches many small fires MODIS misses. Adding them together makes the count jump every time a satellite is launched.

This tool uses one fixed reference, S-NPP VIIRS, so the sensor no longer matters. For the years both sensors flew, a gradient boosting model learned how what MODIS saw in each 11 km cell and month relates to what S-NPP saw. It then rebuilds VIIRS-equivalent counts for 2000 to 2011. When S-NPP has a gap, NOAA-20 fills it, scaled to S-NPP.

How well does it work?

    Keep in mind

    • Years before 2012 are reconstructed from MODIS. Single months are good to about 10 to 20 percent; seasons and years are better.
    • November 2000 to June 2002 rely on Terra alone and are the least certain.
    • Only vegetation fires are counted. Low-confidence detections and persistent industrial heat sources are removed.
    • A single 11 km cell is noisy. Areas of several cells give steadier numbers.
